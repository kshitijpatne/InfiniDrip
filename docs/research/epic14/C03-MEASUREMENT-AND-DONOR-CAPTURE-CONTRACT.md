# C03 — Canonical body, garment, size, and donor-measurement contract

**Status:** Draft for Slice 221 review; not yet the accepted C03 exit.  
**Snapshot:** 2026-09-24.  
**Scope:** Contract and evidence only. No garment code, recipe, supplier contact,
paid standard, hosted service, or physical sampling is authorized here.

## Executive decision

The current seven recipes are deterministic, but their shared `Measurements`
object is not a clean body-measurement dictionary. It contains body dimensions,
finished-garment targets, fit/ease controls, and tank design controls under one
type. The same `length` key means high-point-shoulder-to-hem for tops and
waist-to-hem for a skirt. `sleeveLength` and `inseam` already mean finished
pattern lengths. `ease` is one scalar applied at different body regions in
different recipes. Tank strap width and neckline edits are stored as
measurements even though they are design controls. Defaults and derived values
do not carry field-level provenance.

The future contract must keep five concepts distinct:

1. **Body input:** a named wearer dimension with a body landmark/path and
   capture record.
2. **Finished-garment target:** a desired measurement on the sewn garment,
   chosen or derived through a visible, approved rule.
3. **Pattern value:** coordinates, panel dimensions, seam lengths and marks
   generated by deterministic drafting.
4. **Style/construction control:** user-owned ease, neckline, closure, pocket,
   rise, vent or similar design choice.
5. **Evidence value:** a preset, calculation, supplier statement, or actual
   sample measurement, each kept distinct from user-measured body facts.

These categories must never be inferred from a label alone. A numeric value
needs a meaning, unit, reference frame, source and revision. A derived value
also needs its formula/version and input provenance. Unknown values stay
unknown and block only the outputs that depend on them.

For the user's measurement-first flow, the default output is one explicitly
identified custom size. A graded run remains a separate, opt-in operation and
requires a named grade rule, size labels, per-measure/POM deltas, source or
product rationale, range, review and user approval. The existing XS–XL/M run
is a repository-owned digital rule set, not an approved population chart or a
proof that a custom base is size M. This resolves the maintainer's one-size
first decision; it does not make the existing drafts physically fit-validated.

Photos may begin reference or concept exploration. A photo without a calibrated
scale cannot become a measurement. An assembled donor can be screened with
coached flat measurements, but cut feasibility requires confirmed recovered
panel geometry, orientation, grain/stretch and defect exclusions. A universal
mandatory donor-measurement list would ask for irrelevant data and still miss
the geometry that determines whether a target pattern can be cut.

## Scope, source boundary, and confidence labels

This packet reconciles C01's actual recipe/output audit, C02's standards and
tool evidence, C05's measurement-driven-avatar limits, C06's three upcycling
evidence tiers, and the current source code. C01's inspected PDFs and assembled
renders remain the visual evidence for current outputs. The 2026-09-24 C02
standards page snapshot is used for scope/status only; protected standard
definitions, tables and diagrams are not copied into this contract. ISO
8559-1:2017 is the apparel-specific anthropometric reference for later garment
measurement-guide work; ISO 7250-1 provides a broader technological-design
measurement framework. Their official public scope supports this division,
but neither scope page supplies the full field-level procedure needed here.
The exact landmark guides must come from licensed access or a separately
authored, qualified technical-designer-reviewed guide.

Keep **what a value means** separate from **where it came from**. A body
measurement can be user-captured, imported, or still unresolved; a finished
POM can be a user target, a calculation, or a sample observation. The future
record therefore needs orthogonal `semanticKind` and `provenance` fields:

| Axis | Values required by this product | Evidence and behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `semanticKind` | `BODY_MEASURE`, `GARMENT_MEASURE`, `FINISHED_POM`, `PATTERN_PARAMETER`, `STYLE_CONTROL`, `GRADE_RULE`, `MATERIAL_PROPERTY`, `DONOR_MEASURE`, `PANEL_GEOMETRY`, `VISUAL_OBSERVATION`. | Defines reference frame and units. A value's label alone never determines its kind. |
| `provenance` | `USER_CAPTURED`, `USER_SELECTED`, `PRESET`, `INHERITED`, `CALCULATED`, `SUPPLIER`, `SAMPLE_ACTUAL`, `IMAGE_OBSERVED`, `UNRESOLVED`. | Includes source/revision/date/method as applicable. `UNRESOLVED` is a state, not a source. |
| `evidenceStatus` | `UNCONFIRMED`, `USER_CONFIRMED`, `SOURCE_CONFIRMED`, `SAMPLE_MEASURED`, `CONFLICT`. | No generic green “verified” state. Confirmation does not establish fit or manufacturing readiness. |
| `confidence` | A method-backed assessment only when a validation basis exists. | Store method, validation population/domain and uncertainty basis. Do not invent a percentage, universal threshold, or “high confidence” badge before validation. |

For example, an entered chest girth is `BODY_MEASURE` + `USER_CAPTURED` +
`UNCONFIRMED`; after the user reviews it, only its evidence status changes.
A POM from draft geometry is `FINISHED_POM` + `CALCULATED`, tied to the exact
pattern and formula revision. A tape-measured sample POM is `FINISHED_POM` +
`SAMPLE_ACTUAL`, tied to sample size, material and round. These are not
interchangeable records.

### Required value, provenance and invalid-input rules

For any field required by the selected operation, its dictionary entry must
contain: semantic ID; display label; type; unit; reference frame (body, sewn
garment, flat source garment, recovered panel, or pattern); definition and
landmark/path; capture instructions and state; source/license or product
decision; allowed technical range plus separately labeled warning bands;
confidence/method; dependencies; and an actionable missing, conflict, or
out-of-range response. The raw input must be retained even when invalid so the
person can see and correct it. No silent clamping, substitution, or
autocompletion is allowed.

The current application instead stores a complete numeric `Measurements`
object, initializes it from `STANDARD_M`, and marks an empty numeric edit as
`NaN`. `STANDARD_M` has no linked population table, named source dataset,
measurement event, user, date, helper, repeat or uncertainty. It is a useful
digital fixture/preset, not the current person's measurement record. A future
measurement-first route must begin with missing values (or an explicitly
named preset that remains visibly `PRESET`) and cannot pass a blank required
field by borrowing a standard-M number.

The fixture values are: `neck 40`, `chest 100`, `shoulderWidth 45`, `bicep 38`,
`length 70`, `armholeDepth 24`, `sleeveLength 22`, `waist 84`, `hip 100`,
`hipDepth 20`, `crotchDepth 27`, `thigh 58`, `knee 40`, `inseam 78`, `ease 10`,
`strapWidth 8`, `neckDrop 5`, and `neckWidthEase 0` (all centimetres). These
values are current code defaults only. `STANDARD_M` must not seed an
individualized quick-start silently.

The current green `measurementsPlausible`/“validated” state means only that the
recipe's configured numeric guardrails and applicable ratios pass. It does not
check capture method, landmark accuracy, body shape, garment fit, material
behavior, sample outcome or factory acceptance. Product copy must not reuse that
state as a fit-validation badge.

`controls.ts` exposes each numeric `step` as an increment, but `inputError`
checks finiteness and min/max only; it does not verify that typed input lies on
the displayed step grid. Direct edits are retained. The +/- action is an
explicit correction and may move an invalid value to a bound. Future capture
must preserve entered precision and avoid rounding tape readings to a UI step.

Store an entered value and normalized engine value separately when unit
conversion is used; retain entered unit/precision, conversion rule, method,
measurer choice (self/helper/other source), capture date, source media or
document reference, repeat readings and user correction history. Do not round
an entered tape value to the UI button's step or imply that displayed decimal
places are measurement accuracy. Store calculated values with the exact input
IDs, formula/rule version and output revision. Store actual sample readings in
a separate record tied to style, size, material, sample round and revision.

The exact corrected meanings for the quick route are therefore:

- **Ask as wearer measurements:** only the body facts in the table above whose
  capture definition has been independently reviewed. The current `armholeDepth`
  and lower-body thigh/knee station mappings are not ready to be collected as
  fit-critical body fields.
- **Ask as garment design targets:** top HPS-to-hem, skirt waistline-to-hem,
  sleeve cap-to-hem, trouser finished inseam, neckline/strap geometry, and
  construction options. They must be visually distinct from body input.
- **Calculate and show:** pattern coordinates and finished POMs, with formula,
  rule version and source IDs. Never relabel these calculations as sampled
  garment data.
- **Do not ask in a body-measurement wizard:** body neck circumference for a
  recipe that derives its neckline from chest; default measurement fields the
  selected recipe does not consume; every grade size when the route is one
  custom size; supplier/sample values before those sources exist.

### Missing, conflicting and invalid-data behavior

The proposed app contract uses three explicit states; these are product
decisions for later implementation, not a description of current export
blocking behavior:

| State | Examples | Required interaction and effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `UNRESOLVED` | Required body/target value absent; body-vs-finished meaning conflicts; a photo cannot establish scale/material. | Keep the draft editable if enough data exists for exploration. Never substitute a fixture value. |
| `CONFLICT` | Two repeats disagree; body waist reference differs from garment waistband level; an inherited target conflicts with a new measurement; a source image observation conflicts with user confirmation. | Show both values with source, method and units; ask user to select/remeasure or revise the target. | `INVALID` | Non-finite input, a violated structural invariant, self-intersecting pattern, unmatched seam, or unresolvable component geometry. | Preserve entered value and draft state; show the actual failing dependency and correction options. generic “incomplete” badge:

Advisory ranges (including current code guidance bands) must remain distinguishable
from hard mathematical impossibility, expert-validated technical bounds, and
sample-based acceptance tolerances. A warning does not become a hard gate until
its threshold has an owner, evidence basis, domain, and correction pathway.
Likewise, a successful digital invariant is only that coded invariant passing;
it does not authorize “fits,” “production ready,” “cut feasible,” or
“factory approved.”

## Generated outputs and source trace

All current geometry is deterministic code output in centimetres: named
component/pattern roles, outline edges, internal marks, notches, grainlines,
seam interfaces, allowance rules, digital checks, recipe guidance and POM
functions. `composeBlock`/recipe drafts consume the numeric body/control inputs;
POM values are evaluated from the resulting block. A POM with a specified
tolerance is still a code tolerance; it is not a sourced standard or measured
sample tolerance. The assembled/body drawings and tech-pack PDFs captured in
C01 are generated illustrations/documents, not 3D drape or fit evidence.

The current static BOM and ordered construction notes are repository-owned
defaults. They do not become sourced materials because a pattern was drafted.
Option/fabric-specific variants exist for some recipes, but C01 found broad
material labels and fixed estimated quantities. C04 owns the future field-level
tech-pack contract. Preserve this provenance separation when translating an
input into garment specs:

`BODY_MEASURE + USER_CAPTURED → named deterministic draft rule → PATTERN_PARAMETER + CALCULATED → FINISHED_POM + CALCULATED`

is not the same as

`FINISHED_POM + SAMPLE_ACTUAL → method/size/material/revision-bound sample measurement`.

The first chain can support an inspectable digital draft. It cannot establish
physical fit or approve a factory handoff.

## Grading contract

The existing grade code applies explicit additive increments to `Measurements`
and re-drafts every size. It does not derive grade rules from the user's
measurements, a selected population, a brand chart, a fitted sample, or a
supplier. Rules currently omit some options and POMs; those values consequently
stay constant across sizes. Woven shirt changes nine listed body fields; skirt
changes three; trouser changes seven; upper knit recipes share the Tee rule.

For the future product contract, a graded run requires all of the following
before it can be generated/exported:

1. One explicitly identified base style, base size and measurement revision.
2. A complete grade profile with named size labels and per-size values/rules
   for body dimensions **and** relevant finished-garment POMs/controls.
3. The source/population/brand basis or an explicit user-authored rule with no
   standards-compliance claim.
4. The size interval and declared unsupported edge cases.
5. Deterministic output for each size, cross-size invariants, and a visible
   diff from the base.
6. User review and approval of that grade profile, separate from approval of
   the single custom size.

Without that gate, the output is one size only. Do not silently use the current
XS–XL rule, call a custom user “M,” fill missing size steps, or present the
existing run as a brand/personal fit grade.

## Measurement capture contract and guided flow

The full standards are not licensed into this app. The following is a
**proposed product protocol**, not a claim of ISO/ASTM conformity; each exact
landmark illustration and procedure must be reviewed by a qualified technical
designer before it is called industry-standard.

### Wearer body measurements

The guided flow should:

1. Ask which garment and intended wear position are being drafted. For lower
   garments, record the intended waistband level/rise; “waist” without that
   reference is incomplete.
2. Show one field at a time with a front/side/back diagram, named start/end
   landmarks or circumference path, tape/ruler route, body posture, arm/leg
   state, clothing state, unit and the specific garment constraint it informs.
3. Say whether the value is a body circumference, straight body span, vertical
   depth/path, or finished design target. State whether a value is full
   circumference, flat half-width, or a pattern-derived value.
4. Save a first reading and optional repeat as separate facts. Show the delta;
   ask the user to choose the value or remeasure if they disagree. Do not
   silently average, replace with a size chart, or label one value accurate from
   tape resolution alone. No empirically supported universal repeat-difference
   threshold was established in this packet.
5. Preserve capture method (self/helper/imported), entered and canonical unit,
   captured precision, date, body/garment reference, source photo/document,
   repeat values, user selection and edit history. Avoid unnecessary identity
   data; current work remains local-first.
6. Display a deterministic input-to-output trace: the named draft rule,
   dependent geometry/POMs, formulas, guardrails and unresolved dimensions.
   Let the user manually edit the design and inspect the resulting output.
7. Block only operations that depend on missing/invalid fields. Explain the
   exact missing field and action; preserve the entered value and never clamp
   it. A successful digital check says only that its coded invariant passed.

The proposed measurement session should use a consistent, close-fitting
clothing state; neutral posture; relaxed muscles; and tape placement that follows
the named body path without intentional compression. Circumference paths should
state whether the tape is level/horizontal; straight spans/depths must not be
silently measured by a curved tape path. These are app-protocol proposals that
need technical-designer validation against licensed source definitions. They
must not be copied from a protected standard by guessing.

| Field | Guide must explicitly show | Current gap / precondition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neck | Neck-base endpoints/path, head posture and tape level. | Current comment gives “neck base” only; full accepted standard definition unavailable. Woven shirt only currently consumes it. |
| Chest/bust | Named chest/bust level and horizontal path; tape relation to shoulders/back. | Current comment says “full circumference” only. A neutral label must not imply population or gender. |

## Upcycling and source-garment measurement contract

Source-garment measurements are not wearer body measurements and do not share
their coordinate system. Keep photo, assembled flat and recovered panel evidence
as separate records.

| Tier | May start with | Ask/show | What it can support | Gate / prohibited conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — photo-only exploration** | Photos are optional but can be the first input. Ask for front, back, side, inside/label and damage/detail images only when useful. The user can annotate or manually describe the source and target. | Store original photos and their angle/visible region; mark observations as user-confirmed, visually inferred, or unknown. A ruler/paper grid is useful only when camera/plane calibration is specified. | Reference board, visible-feature checklist, manual idea editing and a next-step guide. | An uncalibrated image is not a scale, body measurement, panel outline, fiber identification, hidden construction record, “will fit”, or cut feasibility. Do not block exploration for missing measurements. |
| **2 — assembled donor screening** | A coached, relaxed flat garment measurement set selected by the actual screening question. Photos remain supporting evidence. | State garment laid flat, fastened/open state, no-pull condition, unit, path endpoints, full vs half width and layer count. Ask only source POMs that compare with named target constraints. Re-measure disagreements visibly. | Approximate size/shape screening and a list of missing evidence/likely alteration constraints. | Not panel geometry, under-seam allowance, usable cuttable area, fiber/strength/stretch recovery, exact yield, sewability or a cut plan. |
| **3 — recovered-panel cut feasibility** | Deconstructed and exposed panels, or a validated traced panel boundary that the user confirms. | Per panel: outline/holes/defect mask, scale and units, front/back/face orientation, grain and stretch directions, seam reserve, fold status, layer/interlining, trims and excluded regions. Record physical panel identity and source garment. | Deterministic 2D target-pattern nesting against the confirmed usable region and a traceable reuse/shortage report. | Without confirmed panel geometry and defect exclusions, no cut-feasibility claim. No unknown fiber content, tensile strength, colorfastness, shrinkage, recovery, or sewing survivability claims without evidence/testing. |

For Tier 2, required fields are the dependency set for one explicit source-to-
target screen. Build that set by tracing the target constraints to the specific
source POMs that can answer them; do not ship one “all tops” or “all bottoms”
checklist as if every value were mandatory. The guided screens should explain
how to place a straightedge/tape, where a flat width starts/ends, whether the
reported figure is full circumference or half-width, what is hidden by seams or
facings, and what the value can establish. For Tier 3, assembled circumferences
do not substitute for individual panel geometry.

The dependency generator must be deterministic and inspectable:

1. Resolve the selected target recipe, exact target pattern revision, intended
   size and target finished POMs.
2. Enumerate which source regions/panels could supply each target piece; show
   the user the proposed reuse map and let them change it.
3. For Tier 2, request only the donor garment's corresponding flat dimensions
   needed to answer the selected screen. For example, a top-to-top dimension
   screen may request flat underarm chest width and HPS-to-hem length; if a
   sleeve is to be retained/altered it may also request sleeve length/width and
   armhole or sleeve seam dimensions. A bottom screen may request fastened
   waistband width, hip width at a named drop from waistband, rise, inseam and
   hem width. These are candidate checks, not universal required sets and not
   panel-cut evidence. Each field is included only when it maps to a named
   target constraint and the source garment exposes that dimension.
4. If the user cannot expose/measure a dependency (for example a hidden seam,
   no matching panel, or no target station), label it `UNRESOLVED`, state the
   exact consequence, and offer the next useful step: another photo, a coached
   flat measurement, opening the seam, tracing panels, or selecting another
   source piece. Never infer a hidden value from a photograph.
5. Tier 3 compares the target pattern's full cut boundary, including its
   required allowance, to each calibrated and user-confirmed usable panel
   polygon. It must preserve holes/defects, folds, selvage, layer count,
   orientation, grain/stretch direction, and excluded regions. A positive
   geometric nesting result is still only material coverage; it does not prove
   sewing survivability, colorfastness, shrinkage or finished fit.

The interface should present each measurement with garment-state photos or
diagrams, a ruler/tape route, fastening and tension state, exact endpoints or
vertical drop, flat/full-width convention and unit. Repeat readings are shown
separately. The user's confirmed value and any inferred visual observation must
remain distinct. The exact field list is therefore generated after the
source-to-target map is chosen; the examples above help the user understand
why a prompt appears without pretending every conversion needs every value.

Research supports the dependency boundary: *Rags2Riches* (SIGGRAPH 2025)
optimizes reuse from modeled source and target designs **and their sewing
patterns**, with panel/seam information; it is not photo-only garment
reconstruction. Its physical example validates the research pipeline's
particular input, not arbitrary photo conversion. Keep the later panel
feasibility result as a deterministic geometric test whose inputs are
user-confirmed.

### External research and primary public scope

- ASTM International, [D5219-25, *Standard Terminology Relating to Body
  Dimensions for Apparel Sizing*](https://store.astm.org/d5219-25.html), public
  scope/version page; full standard is sold separately.
- ASTM International, [D5585-21, body measurements for adult female misses,
  sizes 00–20](https://store.astm.org/d5585-21.html), official scope as recorded
  in C02; population-limited, not a universal table.
- ASTM International, [D6240/D6240M-24a, adult male body measurements, chest
  34–52, short/regular/tall](https://store.astm.org/d6240_d6240m-24a.html),
  official scope page; population/range-limited.
- ISO, [7250-1:2017, Basic human body measurements for technological design —
  body measurement definitions and landmarks](https://www.iso.org/standard/65246.html).
  The ISO page says this edition was reviewed/confirmed in 2023 and describes a
  measurement-definition framework that should be supplemented for the
  application. The full standard remains unlicensed here; this packet does not
  reproduce its definitions, tables or diagrams.
- ISO, [8559-1:2017, Size designation of clothes — Part 1: Anthropometric
  definitions for body measurement](https://www.iso.org/standard/61686.html).
  Its official scope describes an apparel-specific measurement guide for
  population/size profiling, product development and fit mannequins; the page
  reports this edition was reviewed and confirmed in 2026. Use it as a named
  reference only: the full text and its figures are not licensed or copied in
  this packet.
- Gill, S. & Parker, C. J. (2017), “Scan posture definition and hip girth
  measurement: the impact on clothing design and body scanning,” *Ergonomics*,
  60(8), [DOI 10.1080/00140139.2016.1251621](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140139.2016.1251621).
  Their 64-participant comparison found hip girth changes with scan posture and
  that some clothing measurement practices may not capture the largest lower
  body circumference. This supports preserving named path, posture and source
  method as part of a measurement record; it does not supply a universal
  InfiniDrip hip definition or an error threshold.
- Yoon, J. C. & Radwin, R. G. (1994), “The Accuracy of Consumer-Made Body
  Measurements for Women's Mail-Order Clothing,” *Human Factors*, 36(3),
  [DOI 10.1177/001872089403600311](https://doi.org/10.1177/001872089403600311).
  The study's 103-woman sample and method/error limits are summarized above;
  do not extrapolate it into modern population-wide measurement tolerances.
- Lee, Y. T. T. (1994), [NISTIR 5411, *Body Dimensions for
  Apparel*](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.5411), a historical compilation
  comparing five different sources and organizing 91 apparel body dimensions;
  it explicitly records that sources used differing measurement methods,
  reference systems and target populations. It is useful schema/history
  evidence, not a modern population dataset or a substitute for a current
  approved measurement protocol.
- McKinney, E., Gill, S., Dorie, A. & Roth, S. (2017), “Body-to-Pattern
  Relationships in Women’s Trouser Drafting Methods: Implications for Apparel
  Mass Customization,” *Clothing and Textiles Research Journal*, 35(1),
  [DOI 10.1177/0887302X16664406](https://doi.org/10.1177/0887302X16664406).
  The authors report inconsistent body-to-pattern measurement/shape
  relationships across two methods and differently shaped bodies, citing
  proportional non-corresponding inputs, standard values, ease choices and
  body-shape omissions among the causes. This supports keeping trouser
  station mappings explicit; it does not validate InfiniDrip's current offsets.
- Yan, S., Wirta, J. & Kämäräinen, J.-K. (2019), “Anthropometric clothing
  measurements from 3D body scans,” *Machine Vision and Applications*,
  [DOI 10.1007/s00138-019-01054-4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00138-019-01054-4).
  Their pipeline uses a commercial scanner, a fitted SMPL body model,
  non-rigid ICP, multiple circumference paths and a learned regressor; the
  NOMO3D study contains 194 male and 181 female subjects and reports
  measurement-specific MAE from 2.5 to 16.0 mm. Accuracy depends on this
  dataset, scanner, fitting and evaluation; it does not establish accuracy for
  a few typed dimensions or the current 2D app.
- Parker, C. J., Gill, S., Harwood, A., Hayes, S. G. & Ahmed, M. (2022), “A
  method for increasing 3D body scanning's precision: Gryphon and consecutive
  scanning,” *Ergonomics*, 65(1),
  [DOI 10.1080/00140139.2021.1931473](https://doi.org/10.1080/00140139.2021.1931473).
  Its public abstract describes 97 participants, 121 measurements, and a
  five-scan filtering method; the abstract and practitioner summary give
  slightly different headline percentages (97.5% vs 97.3%). Treat the result
  as evidence that capture protocol/repeats matter, not as an InfiniDrip
  accuracy target or transferable universal threshold.
- Korosteleva et al. (2025), [Rags2Riches project and paper](https://korosteleva.com/publication/rags2riches/),
  panel/pattern-based reuse, not uncalibrated photo-only conversion.
