# Codex review — *Fashion Upcycling*

## Source identity and limits

- **Visible title/author:** *Fashion Upcycling: The DIY Guide to Sewing, Mending, and Sustainably Reinventing Your Wardrobe*, Ysabel Hilado; confirmed from the cover and title/copyright pages.
- **Edition:** first edition / first printing, August 2023; ©2023 Rocky Nook; ISBN 979-8-88814-033-8. The PDF metadata has no reliable title/author fields; it identifies a `pdftk-java`/iText producer and a 2025 file modification date. File provenance and reproduction rights were not assessed.
- **Pagination:** the supplied PDF has 612 pages. Printed folio numbers are not visible on the inspected pages, so references below use zero-based-independent PDF page indices as printed by the PDF reader (1-based). Do not mistake these indices for book folios.
- I visually inspected the cover/title/copyright/contents (PDF pp. 1, 3, 7–8) and every cited instructional page below. No pages or images are copied into this report.

## Findings

### Direct book instruction, not product-specific evidence

- **Practice swatches:** PDF p. 33, heading “Practice Sewing Samples,” recommends practicing a new technique on muslin or another fabric similar to the intended material before using the main fabric. The page is a text/photo introduction; it does not prescribe a digital verification rule.
- **Seam and topstitch sequence:** PDF pp. 64–73 show and describe putting right sides together, finishing raw edges by serging or zigzag, marking a seam allowance, pinning and sewing the line, pressing the allowance open, and placing topstitching on the visible side. PDF p. 67 gives a half-inch example seam allowance and says to adjust it where needed; this is a tutorial example, not a universal project rule. I visually checked the photo sequence on pp. 64, 67, 71–72 and the follow-on step at p. 73.
- **Refashioned tank pattern:** PDF pp. 120–132 describe tracing a favorite tank to create a basic pattern for converting a T-shirt. The example assigns different seam allowances to side/hem edges and armhole/neckline edges (p. 125), then recommends trying on the altered tank and adjusting shoulder/side seams (p. 132). This is a hands-on refashioning example, not a complete validated drafting method or a claim of predictable fit. The p. 125 tracing/seam-allowance photo and p. 132 finishing/try-on text were visually inspected.
- **Closures and adjustability:** PDF p. 587 suggests experimenting with zipper, lace-up, snap, ring, drawstring, elastic, buckle, and button details. These are design examples; they do not define component geometry, attachment order, tolerances, or a production standard.
- **Material-dependent preparation and finishing:** PDF pp. 588–590 discuss prewashing to observe fabric change, choosing needle size/material appropriately, sampling before cutting, and serging or zigzagging edges prone to fraying. These are general sewing cautions; they do not establish safe machine settings or fabric behavior for a specific InfiniDrip draft.

The table of contents identifies 25 refashioning projects, including a patchwork top, T-shirt-to-tank conversion, an adjustable drawstring button-up, and altered trousers (PDF pp. 7–8). This supports the book’s broad role as a photo-led beginner upcycling/tutorial resource, rather than an industrial pattern or technical-flat reference.

## G04 blocker assessment

- **S454 Woven front:** no cited figure resolves whether this product should hide the source-joined neckline beneath the actual collar-leaf fills. The seam sequence says nothing about this source-specific displayed layer order. It neither supersedes S425 nor approves the S454 mask.
- **S442 Woven back:** no cited figure describes a paired-sleeve projection or a detached band placement that clears the specific crossing while keeping the S370 side-gutter detail positions fixed. A generic band/closure or sewing step cannot supply that relation.
- **G04 generally:** the basic seam/topstitch, seam-allowance, closure and explicit “sample before cutting” concepts may inform future optional construction education and/or technique-card content. They cannot be copied into universal settings: the book itself presents material/project-specific examples, and G04 requires source-derived facts and honest omissions.

## Wider product relevance

Potential later use is a user-led refashion/repair learning path: guide users through reusing an existing garment, show reversible closure/customization choices, and link construction concepts (seam, topstitch, raw-edge finish) to concise illustrated learning material. Its “try on and adjust” steps can inform educational language only; InfiniDrip cannot claim digital fit validation from them. A sustainability/material-reuse feature would need a separately admitted scope and its own reviewed requirements. No feature, roadmap item, physical-validation path or G04 acceptance change is proposed by this source review.
