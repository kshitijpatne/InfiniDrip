# Codex review — four additional supplied sewing and pattern books

## Method and source limits

Reviewed the supplied PDFs locally using Poppler text extraction, rendered selected pages to temporary files outside the repository, and visually inspected every page cited below. No page images or extended text are reproduced here. PDF page indices are 1-based; printed folios are stated only where visible or confirmed by the book's own contents. The user-supplied files' distribution provenance and permissions have not been established. Searchable text and scan/OCR quality vary by file.

## *A Guide to Fashion Sewing*, Sixth Edition — Connie Amaden-Crawford

- **Identity:** The supplied filename attributes the work to Connie Amaden-Crawford. PDF metadata has no reliable title/author fields. The visible preface at PDF p. 1 names *A Guide to Fashion Sewing, Sixth Edition* and describes industry design-room sewing methods. A separate title/copyright leaf was not present in the reviewed opening pages; author/publisher/edition provenance is therefore only partly verifiable from this file.
- **Direct book instruction:** PDF p. 339 distinguishes a detached cuff (a separate fabric piece attached at the sleeve edge, optionally controlling gathers/pleats) from a fold-up cuff, and illustrates cuff/placket forms. PDF pp. 347 and 350 show sleeve assembly and a shirt sleeve-placket sewing sequence. PDF p. 133 explains that garment assembly order can keep the sewn garment flat while practical construction proceeds. Rendered images were visually inspected.
- **G04 assessment:** This is useful physical-construction context for sleeve bands/cuffs, sewing sequence, seams, and technical terminology. It does not prescribe a digital flat's orientation or position for paired detached band details. It does not prove an over/under or rotation for the current Woven sleeves, nor a front collar/neckline visibility rule. Its physical cuff assembly is not evidence for source-specific view semantics.
- **Broader product value:** Potential reference for later construction education and technique explanations. Do not turn examples into universal settings or product validation.

## *High-Fashion Sewing Secrets from the World's Best Designers* — Claire B. Shaeffer

- **Identity:** PDF metadata identifies Claire B. Shaeffer and the fuller title. The visible opening/title material and copyright page identify a 1997 copyright; the scan has a public-library stamp and Internet Archive markings. This describes the scanned copy, not the user's file provenance or permission to redistribute it.
- **Direct book instruction:** The contents at PDF p. 8 (printed folio vii) list collar, lapel, placket, fastening, pocket, and hem techniques. The collar chapter's tie-collar instructions and diagrams at PDF pp. 83–85 (printed pp. 71–73) show a specific flat-pattern tie collar, neckline preparation, collar attachment, clipping, and finishing. The placket chapter begins at PDF p. 100 (printed p. 88) with a keyhole-opening example. These pages were rendered and visually inspected.
- **G04 assessment:** These examples distinguish collar/pattern pieces and show a generic construction order, but they describe another collar design and do not establish that the Woven source's neckline seam should be masked by its projected collar leaves. They contain no source-bound sleeve-pair orientation or fixed-S370 band-placement rule.
- **Broader product value:** Could inform later optional construction education for collar/placket techniques. It is a technique collection, not an industrial CAD exchange specification or evidence of this product's recipe topology.

## *How to Use, Adapt, and Design Sewing Patterns: From Store-Bought Patterns to Drafting Your Own* — Lee Hollahan

- **Identity/provenance:** The supplied filename names this book. Its PDF metadata instead says title “How to,” author “Debby-Lee Ellis,” and 2011; the title/copyright leaf is absent from the supplied scan. The visible contents and “About this book” pages (PDF pp. 1–3) describe the named pattern-use/adaptation subject and match its chapter structure. Google Books and library catalog records corroborate a Lee Hollahan book with this title, but cannot prove that this particular scan is the same complete edition. Treat the binary's bibliographic identity as probable, not verified; its provenance/rights are unknown.
- **Direct book instruction:** The contents at PDF p. 1 list sleeve/cuff styling, collar styles, pattern blocks, and pattern design. The visually inspected collar page at PDF p. 87 (contents identify the corresponding printed topic as p. 100) explains collar-edge/stand/roll-line terms, measuring a neckline, and top/under-collar pattern relationships with diagrams. PDF p. 75 (contents identify sleeve/cuff styling at printed p. 88) shows sleeve-block adaptation and sleeve shaping. The paired pattern-block layouts at PDF pp. 100–101 show colored pieces with grainline/fold directions. The relevant pages were visually inspected.
- **G04 assessment:** This is the most directly relevant supplied book for general collar terminology and pattern-piece relationships, but it still does not identify the correct visibility or layer order for the exact `live-draft:woven-shirt:14:14` edges, nor a view projection that fits the paired Woven sleeves while preserving S370 band positions. Fabric cutting layout is not technical-flat composition.
- **Broader product value:** Potential later pattern literacy, adaptation, collar/sleeve terminology, and educational guidance. Before using this scan as a durable bibliographic source, replace its uncertain identity/provenance with a verified publisher/library copy.

The shown shirt and pattern layouts are not evidence about InfiniDrip's current source topology.

## Combined conclusion

The four books add general physical construction, collar/sleeve terminology, pattern-layout, fitting, and education context. Hollahan's collar page is the closest match to the subject, and Amaden-Crawford/Mahon show detached cuff and shirt-component examples. None supplies the missing source-specific fact that would truthfully resolve the S454 front seam contact while retaining S425, or the S442 Woven back crossing while keeping the exact S370 band positions. They do not change the current G04 next action or justify relaxing holds or acceptance. Keep the Woven fronts and backs held and all-seven V02 unchanged.
